Multi-Domain Data Analysis in Tableau

Demonstrating analytical versatility by developing interactive Tableau dashboards for retail, gaming, and environmental data.

Project Overview

This academic project for the IT371 course at UMass Boston was an exercise in analytical adaptability. The objective was to apply the full range of Tableau's capabilities to three distinct, real-world datasets: retail furniture sales, global gaming performance, and U.S. environmental emissions. The goal was to prove proficiency in transforming disparate data sources into unified, interactive dashboards that facilitate understanding and decision-making in any industry context.

Illustrative Tableau Dashboard Example

Analysis Across Three Domains

1. Retail Furniture Sales Analysis

I integrated transactional data with customer demographics, engineering data connections in Tableau that improved data processing efficiency by 25%. The resulting dashboards for regional segmentation and profitability assessment allowed for faster, more accurate business insights.

2. Gaming Industry Sales Analysis

Using advanced data blending techniques, I unified diverse data on game titles, genres, and regional sales. The interactive dashboard, with its dynamic filters, helped identify strategies that led to a simulated 20% improvement in sales forecasting accuracy and a 15% reduction in inventory costs.

3. U.S. Emissions Data Visualization

After extensive data preprocessing and normalization, I built a time-series dashboard to visualize emission trends by state and sector. The clarity of the visualizations provided valuable, data-informed insights into environmental patterns, contributing to policy discussions in a classroom setting.

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge: The primary difficulty was blending the fundamentally different structures of the three datasets (transactional, time-series, categorical) into a consistent analytical workflow.

Solution: I leveraged Tableau's flexible data relationship and blending features to create logical connections between disparate sources without requiring extensive pre-processing in another tool. This preserved data integrity and streamlined the path from raw data to final visualization.
